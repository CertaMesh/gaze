# v0.12 Kiji Decoder Scorecard

This report measures PR #425 (branch `agent/kiji-label-registry-fail-closed-main`):
the Kiji DistilBERT safety net now decodes classifier ids with one label
registry pinned to the upstream model card, all in-process runtimes (ORT,
Tract, Candle) and the Python subprocess runner share that registry and one
BIO decoder, and every backend fails the whole request closed on malformed
classifier output. It supersedes the first characterization of this branch
(BASE `963773c` → CANDIDATE `c49940c`), which was measured before the root
cause of the Kiji-cell movement was known and against a pre-#423 basis.

This is evidence only: no accepted baseline was installed or modified, and
`--accept-baseline` was not used.

## Root cause of the Kiji-cell movement

Before this PR the in-process Kiji decoders on `main` (`backend/ort.rs` and
`backend/decode.rs`) mapped classifier ids 3–6 as `B-LOC, I-LOC, B-ORG, I-ORG`,
and the subprocess runner (`scripts/bench/kiji-runner.py`) used a third,
MISC-first order. The pinned bundle's classifier head actually emits:

| id | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| label | `O` | `B-PER` | `I-PER` | `B-ORG` | `I-ORG` | `B-LOC` | `I-LOC` | `B-MISC` | `I-MISC` |

Sources for that order:

- Upstream model card `config.json` for `onnx-community/distilbert-NER-ONNX` at
  the pinned commit `3a19fe9404a4469d91aa3d551558a97f68872f67`
  (`https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/distilbert-NER-ONNX/raw/3a19fe9404a4469d91aa3d551558a97f68872f67/config.json`,
  SHA-256 `f109facddb205dac712adf5877e4315fae62041bc0916fe808d92abdb594d1fe`),
  `id2label = {0:O, 1:B-PER, 2:I-PER, 3:B-ORG, 4:I-ORG, 5:B-LOC, 6:I-LOC, 7:B-MISC, 8:I-MISC}`.
- Empirical probe of the pinned fp32 bundle (`model.onnx` from bundle SHA-256
  `c129e135d86698e67c4836456212666f94a56ceaf995acd60532f557b3120d2f`, run through
  onnxruntime with the bundle tokenizer, argmax per token): city names → id 5,
  company and institution names → ids 3/4, person names → ids 1/2, event names
  → ids 7/8.
- `crates/gaze-recognizers/src/safety_net/kiji_distilbert/backend/decode.rs`,
  test module `label_registry_parity`: a verbatim copy of the pre-unification
  ORT decoder driven by the upstream `id2label` matches the shared decoder
  span-for-span on a synthetic tensor covering every id 0..8; driven by the old
  permuted table it differs from the shared decoder at exactly the id 3–6 spans
  as a `location`↔`organization` swap and nowhere else.

The bundle ships no `config.json` or other `id2label` artifact
(`SHA256SUMS`, `labels.json` (vocabulary only), `model.onnx`,
`tokenizer.json`), so the registry cannot be re-verified at bundle load; the
SHA-256 bundle pin plus the parity tests are the guard.

Effect on `main`: every Kiji suspect that is really a place was reported as
`organization` (mapped to the `Name` safety-net class) and every organisation
as `location`. `PER` and `MISC` were correct. Span positions and scores were
unaffected. Defect record: solo todo #2925; first shipped in v0.9.0-rc.1
(ORT/decode: `4b8db66`, PR #250; runner: `c5e623f`, PR #236).

## Document-level attribution of the restore and FP movement

Method: the same 2,910-document population was driven through
`clean_for_bench --config full-stack-kiji-resolve` on both heads (identical
environment to the scorecard) and, per document, only metadata was retained:
`restore` outcome, initial `LeakSuspect` offsets/class/kind, the final
protection trace (raw offsets, class, action, decision), manifest-integrity
counters, and byte metrics. No corpus text was stored or reported.

**Restore accounting reconciles exactly to the fallback.** On BASE, 557
documents restore inexactly and 557 documents carry at least one
`fallback_redact` action; on CANDIDATE, 629 and 629. On both halves, zero
documents restore inexactly without a fallback-redact action, manifest-integrity
errors are zero, and the five documents with a failed restore decision are the
same five on both halves (pre-existing `unknown_token`/`manifest_bypass` cases,
unrelated to Kiji). No manifest or restore defect is present; every restore
failure is the one-way `SafetyNetFallback::Redact` outcome.

**The flips.** 86 documents go exact → inexact and 14 go inexact → exact
(net +72). In all 100 the initial Kiji suspects are geometrically identical on
both halves; only their class differs (the LOC/ORG swap), and the fallback
count goes 0 → N or N → 0. The 86 losses are 76 DE / 10 EN, and 35 of them are
negative-corpus documents with no gold PII, where the redaction protects
nothing and is pure FP and restore cost; the 86 documents carry 383 gold
entities, the 14 recoveries 112.

**Mechanism.** The initial resolve decisions are class-independent except for
`LeakKind::ClassMismatch` handling, and the initial mismatch counts are equal
in every flipped document, so the resolve phase itself behaves identically.
What differs is the class passed to
`tokenize_with_family("safety_net", &suspect.class, …)`: correctly labelled
suspects now mint `Location`-class tokens where `main` minted `Name`-class
tokens and vice versa. The follow-up safety-net scan runs Kiji over that
re-tokenized text, and its verdict on the different placeholder tokens differs;
where it returns a residual or class-mismatching suspect the request falls
back to `Redact` over the initial report (`FallbackReason::ResidualSuspect` or
`OverlapConflict`), which is fail-closed by design and one-way by design.
Across the population the class swap re-labels the initial suspects from
`name` 11,410 / `location` 14,298 on BASE to 23,085 / 2,622 on CANDIDATE; the
follow-up scan tips 100 documents across the fallback boundary and changes the
fallback-redaction span set of 622 documents.

**Confinement.** All +2,866 FP bytes, all −105 leaked bytes, and the whole
−2 net fully-covered-entity delta occur inside those 622 documents; outside
them not a single byte or entity moves. The `class_coverage_loss` gate
therefore reflects redaction-versus-tokenization geometry in fallback
documents, not a per-class detection regression.

**Assessment.** Against the north star this is an axis-4 (trust) and axis-1
(precision) fix with a quantified axis-2 cost: 72 more documents per 2,910
receive one-way redaction under `SafetyNetMode::Resolve` +
`SafetyNetFallback::Redact` because the follow-up scan now judges correctly
labelled tokens. That fallback is the intended fail-closed behaviour; the cost
is disclosed here rather than traded away, and the earlier `main` numbers were
only lower because a wrong label happened to satisfy the follow-up scan more
often. Reducing fallback frequency for correctly labelled suspects is
follow-up work on the resolve/follow-up contract, not on the decoder.

Release readiness is false on both halves, as expected. Each half reports the
same ten zero-target gate names. No production readiness claim is made.
